## [Obituaries](https://stratechery.com/2026/obituaries/) (2026-11-16, score 62, free)

**Thesis:** Jensen Huang resigned as NVIDIA CEO on Thursday 2026-11-12. The trigger was affairs, including one with an executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore data-centre firm alleged to be smuggling chips to China. Thompson argues the real cause is that NVIDIA came to be treated as a defense contractor and Huang never adjusted. He writes "obituaries" for Huang's tenure, NVIDIA's dominance (2024-2026), the AI bubble (2025-2027?), tech-company independence from government, and American exceptionalism.

- **NVIDIA (Compute leader):** Thompson says NVIDIA's margin durability was "already an open question". CUDA's software moat erodes as developers build on LLM APIs and LLMs write code. Networking/systems is the more durable moat but Google (and so Anthropic) challenges it. The supply-chain moat rests on Huang personally: he "has never signed a contract with TSMC". With 60 direct reports and no succession plan, the "exoskeleton" around Huang may collapse. **Inference:** a key-person and governance shock for NVDA. It also adds regulatory risk: Treasury Secretary Bessent (quoted via the FT) wants an external CEO and board answers "in the next 10 days", and says NVIDIA should be held to bank-like compliance standards.
- **H1 (credit first):** this is the most directly relevant part. Thompson describes "Nvidia's Risky Business": using its cash flow and credit rating to backstop and fund neocloud buyers. He says those bets are "unlikely to continue" without Huang, and that "The neoclouds should be very nervous about losing their patron". If AI revenue (he puts the need at roughly $1tn industry-wide) falls short, hyperscalers will put their own compute first and be seen as "a safe haven", and neocloud assets could flood the market. **Inference:** this is the H1 mechanism almost word for word: leveraged builders (CRWV, NBIS, IREN, ORCL) lose a funding backstop while cash-rich hyperscalers (GOOGL) gain relative standing. Watch H1.T1 (leveraged ND/EBITDA, HY OAS) and H1.T2 (drawdown) over the next few weeks for a reaction.
- **Funding sequence / Alphabet:** "The first part of the AI buildout was funded by free cash flow", then debt in H2 2025, and now equity, "starting with Google this past summer". He says hyperscalers are best placed to get through that progression and to challenge NVIDIA's chip hold. **Inference:** supportive of GOOGL's position in H1 (and indirectly of Broadcom, its TPU partner), though equity issuance by Google is a claim worth checking against filings.
- **TSMC and the Asian supply chain:** Huang was "a one-person coordination function", locking up supplier output with a handshake. **Inference:** NVIDIA's allocation at TSMC and the memory makers (SK Hynix, Samsung, Sandisk) may become less certain. Hyperscaler custom silicon (TPU via AVGO) could get a bigger share of capacity. This is possible reallocation rather than less demand for the bottleneck names, so it isn't clearly negative for the Bottleneck group.
- **China and export controls:** Thompson says China historically made up "about a quarter of Nvidia's sales", China is banning H20 imports to build domestic demand, and "the doomers and the hawks have won" on AI policy. **Inference:** tighter enforcement (Singapore loophole, Commerce blacklist) is a further headwind to NVDA revenue and a tailwind for Chinese alternatives over the long run. This is a policy view, not data.
- **H2 (rates/yen carry):** nothing on rates, JGBs or the yen. **Inference:** a sharp de-rating of the market's largest company could act as the kind of crowded-position unwind H2 describes. If so it would come from idiosyncratic risk, not rates, so it should not be counted as H2 evidence.
